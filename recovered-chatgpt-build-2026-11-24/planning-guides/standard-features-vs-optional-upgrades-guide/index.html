Standard Features vs Optional Upgrades Guide

Separate what is included from what costs extra so you can compare buildings and proposals more accurately.

PART 02

Standard Means the Complete Weather-Tight Exterior Shell

Standard pricing should be understood as the complete exterior building shell: foundation beams, floor system, framed walls, roof system, siding, roofing, standard windows and doors, trim, vents, exterior weatherproofing, and the applicable structural warranty. Options and upgrades are separate selections that change comfort, utility, finish, access, storage, exterior character, site work, or future-use readiness.

PART 03

Decide the Hard-to-Change Items First

Scope clarity is also about timing. The least glamorous decisions are often the ones that become most expensive or impractical to change later.

Site, Access & Drainage

Confirm grade, access path, staging room, drainage, soil conditions, utilities, HOA concerns, and placement before choosing finish upgrades.

Foundation, Floor & Wall System

Confirm load, moisture, framing, wall, and subfloor strategy before discussing finished flooring, workshop loads, or comfort packages.

Roof, Openings & Weather Envelope

Confirm the roof system, pitch, ventilation, doors, windows, skylights, overhangs, and weatherproofing before the shell is framed.

Utility & Comfort Readiness

Electrical, insulation, HVAC, plumbing readiness, and interior finishes should be planned before wall closure and finished surfaces.

PART 04

What Belongs in the Standard Weather-Tight Exterior Shell

The standard build is the complete exterior structure, not a partially described box. It includes the systems that make the building stand, shed water, protect the interior, open and close properly, and arrive as a usable exterior-finished backyard structure.

Roofing, Siding & Exterior Protection

2×6 rafters or engineered roof framing with a premium standard roof profile where model-specific; architectural shingles with available color choices; LP SmartSide vertical siding as the standard exterior siding system unless a selected model or upgrade states otherwise; plus trim, flashing details, drip edges, and related weatherproofing.

Doors, Windows & Structural Warranty

Standard doors, standard windows with screens where applicable, exterior trim around openings, ventilation components, and the stated structural warranty subject to its terms, maintenance responsibilities, and exclusions.

Electrical, insulation, drywall, finished interiors, plumbing readiness, premium doors, additional windows, porches, specialty siding, built-ins, and comfort systems all depend on the quality of the shell underneath. A strong standard build gives those upgrades a better platform.

PART 05

What Belongs in the Optional Upgrade Conversation

An optional upgrade changes the building beyond the standard weather-tight exterior shell. Some upgrades are visual. Others affect structure, comfort, utility, site work, permits, licensed trades, or future finish readiness.

PART 06

What Should Never Be Hidden Inside “Standard” or “Optional” Language

Vague language creates mistrust. Specific language lets you compare projects on real scope rather than a starting price alone.

Unclear Site Work

If the site needs grading, drainage correction, clearing, gravel work, concrete, or unusual access planning, it should be discussed separately and priced clearly.

Blurred Shell vs Interior

A weather-tight exterior shell is not the same as a finished office, studio, guest-ready space, or conditioned room.

Casual Utility Language

Electrical, HVAC, and plumbing involve safety, code, licensed trades, and future-use planning. They should not be treated as casual add-ons.

Upgrade Pressure

A homeowner using a building for simple storage should not be pushed into finished-space upgrades that do not match the intended use.

PART 07

How to Compare Two Shed Quotes Without Being Misled

A fair comparison requires more than matching size. Compare the actual systems, inclusions, exclusions, warranty, and change-order process.

PART 08

A Premium Scope Should Be Documented Before the Build Starts

Clear documentation protects both homeowner and builder. It reduces assumptions and gives both parties the same understanding of the project.

Builder Documentation

Base building model, size, standard material package, exterior features, and warranty terms.

Selected upgrades with product type, placement, quantity, finish, and pricing.

Homeowner & Site Information

Access path, grade, drainage, staging area, and overhead obstructions.

Utility marking and known site constraints.

PART 09

Questions That Expose the Real Scope Behind the Price

What Exactly Is Included?

Ask the builder to identify the foundation, floor, wall, roof, siding, roofing, doors, windows, vents, trim, exterior weatherproofing, and warranty included in the quoted price.

What Exactly Is Excluded?

Ask specifically about site preparation, electrical, insulation, plumbing, concrete, permits, finished interiors, paint or stain responsibilities, and difficult access conditions.

Which Decisions Must Be Made Early?

Clarify any choice affecting framing, openings, roof design, utilities, wall closure, site work, or material ordering before construction begins.

How Are Changes Approved?

Confirm that changes affecting price, scope, materials, or timing will be documented and approved before additional work proceeds.

PART 11

Site Conditions Can Change What Belongs in the Project Scope

Backyard buildings in the Cincinnati Tri-State are not installed on identical sites. Grade, drainage, access, soils, utility locations, neighborhood requirements, and intended use can change what needs to be discussed before final scope.

Special grading, drainage correction, difficult access, concrete work, clearing, unusual bearing conditions, or utility conflicts should be identified separately rather than quietly absorbed into vague “standard installation” language.

A storage shed, workshop, backyard office, studio, hobby building, and pool building can require very different decisions about loads, openings, insulation, climate planning, utilities, and finish readiness.

PART 12

Construction Experience Behind the Guide

This guide is intended to help homeowners compare backyard-building proposals using real construction scope rather than price alone. The best comparison is one that identifies the structural systems, weather envelope, options, exclusions, site assumptions, warranty, and written change process.

IMPORTANT PROJECT DISTINCTION

How this relates to an Appalachian Sheds project

Built on your property

All Appalachian Sheds buildings are constructed on site. Property access, drainage, topography, location restrictions and the selected model can affect planning.

Exterior shell versus finished interior

Our standard building proposal describes a weather-tight exterior shell. Insulation, electrical, plumbing, heating, cooling, and interior wall or ceiling finishes are separate work unless expressly listed in the written scope.

Only the written scope is authoritative

Model-specific specifications, pricing, selected options, finishing requirements and any engineering or permit obligations must be confirmed for the actual building and site. Avoid treating this general guide as an engineering design or a permit determination.

CURRENT PLANNING GUIDANCE

What a standard ASI building includes

Standard pricing is for a building constructed on site as an exterior weather-tight shell. An insulated, electrically wired, climate-controlled, painted, stained or interior-finished building is not implied by a sample photograph or use-case description.

For traditional models, approved standard features include the baseline structural system, two 24×30-inch aluminum single-hung windows, and site-built 60×80-inch Craftsman-style double doors, subject to the model-specific written proposal.

LP siding upgrades, painting and staining, interior finishes, electrical, HVAC and other requested changes must be distinguished from base inclusions before an option price or schedule is represented as final.

PART 01

Start With Passive Moisture Protection — Then Add Mechanical Control Only Where the Building Use Requires It.

For indoor spaces, EPA guidance is to keep relative humidity below 60 percent, with roughly 30–50 percent as the preferred general range where practical.

Vented Roof Assembly

For roofs intentionally designed to be vented, low intake and high exhaust should be coordinated so air can move through a clear ventilation path.

Gable / Supplemental Passive Venting

Gable vents can provide useful passive airflow in simple storage or workshop buildings, but they should not be mixed randomly with other roof-vent strategies.

Powered or solar exhaust can help with heat or process loads when replacement air is deliberately provided. A fan cannot exhaust effectively without makeup air.

PART 02

Dehumidification

Dehumidifiers directly remove moisture from indoor air and can operate even when cooling demand is low. This can be important in mixed-humid summer conditions.

An ERV exchanges stale indoor air with outdoor air while recovering some heat and moisture energy. It improves controlled ventilation but does not automatically replace a dehumidifier.

Wall Vapor-Control Planning

WRB, insulation, air sealing and interior vapor-control layers should be selected as one wall assembly. No universal interior polyethylene rule is applied across all ASI projects.

Humidity / IAQ Monitoring

A simple hygrometer or connected monitor can show whether the building is actually staying within the intended temperature and humidity range.

Choose Either a Vented Roof Strategy or a Properly Designed Unvented Roof Strategy

Balanced ridge-and-soffit ventilation is appropriate for a roof assembly intentionally designed with an air channel from low intake to high exhaust. Spray foam installed directly at the underside of the roof deck can create an unvented roof assembly instead. The two approaches should not be casually combined.

PART 03

Ventilation Must Match the Insulation Location.

If insulation is at the ceiling plane, the roof space may be vented. If the thermal / air-control layer is moved to the roof deck, the design may instead be unvented.

These ranges reflect small-building mobilization, equipment, ordinary installation and coordination. Final cost depends on roof geometry, access, electrical work, drain routing, wall closure and whether equipment is owner-supplied or contractor-installed.

The Standard 10-Mil Ground Barrier Is Already in the ASI Pad Scope.

It should not appear here as a second charge. Only enhanced or project-specific ground-vapor assemblies are additional.

ERV pricing is for controlled fresh-air equipment. ERV operation can reduce ventilation energy and latent load, but a dehumidifier may still be needed when indoor humidity remains high.

Storage, Workshops and Finished Offices Should Not Receive the Same Moisture-Control Package

The more airtight, insulated, occupied and finish-sensitive the building becomes, the more deliberate the humidity and fresh-air strategy should be.

PART 04

Do Not Overbuild a Storage Shed — and Do Not Underbuild an Office.

Use the simplest system that correctly manages the actual building.

Drying Potential Matters.

A wall sealed too aggressively on both sides can create a moisture trap. Final vapor-control direction follows the selected insulation, siding / WRB assembly and project jurisdiction.

Flashing, siding clearances, WRB where required and properly detailed openings handle bulk water before vapor-control questions begin.

PART 05

Ventilation & Moisture Control and HVAC Work Together — But They Are Not the Same Category

Mini-splits control sensible heating and cooling. Dehumidifiers control latent moisture. ERVs provide controlled fresh-air exchange. The building enclosure determines how much work those mechanical systems must do.

Design the Moisture Strategy Before the Building Is Closed

Appalachian Sheds can coordinate the prepared pad, roof assembly, insulation, air sealing, humidity control, fresh-air equipment and monitoring so the building has a deliberate drying and comfort strategy instead of a collection of unrelated vents and devices.

IMPORTANT PROJECT DISTINCTION

How this relates to an Appalachian Sheds project

Built on your property

All Appalachian Sheds buildings are constructed on site. Property access, drainage, topography, location restrictions and the selected model can affect planning.

Exterior shell versus finished interior

Our standard building proposal describes a weather-tight exterior shell. Insulation, electrical, plumbing, heating, cooling, and interior wall or ceiling finishes are separate work unless expressly listed in the written scope.

Only the written scope is authoritative

Model-specific specifications, pricing, selected options, finishing requirements and any engineering or permit obligations must be confirmed for the actual building and site. Avoid treating this general guide as an engineering design or a permit determination.

CURRENT PLANNING GUIDANCE

Ventilation must match the specific roof and use

Traditional ASI construction includes a continuous ridge and soffit ventilation strategy. Specialty mono-slope or other non-traditional roof assemblies require a model-specific ventilation and moisture-control design.

For a finished or conditioned interior, insulation, air sealing, vapor behavior, mechanical ventilation and equipment needs must be evaluated as a complete assembly—not as an isolated vent upgrade.

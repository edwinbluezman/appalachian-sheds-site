HOMEOWNER GUIDE 01 · PROPERTY & SITE

Backyard Building Site Preparation Guide

A dependable building begins with understanding the property—not simply choosing a spot that looks level.

THE ESSENTIAL ANSWER

What should you know?

Confirm the location, drainage, ground stability, access, buried utilities and local restrictions before committing to a building footprint or preparing a base. Site conditions determine the appropriate preparation and may affect the written scope.

PART 01

Start with a property walk-through

After a rainfall

Look for standing water, downspout discharge, low points, erosion and places where water travels toward the proposed building. Do not assume that a gravel base alone will solve a recurring drainage problem.

Check slope and subgrade

Identify organic topsoil, existing fill, roots, soft areas and grade changes. The required excavation, base and compaction depend on what is actually present—not a universal depth specification.

Plan the work area

Consider gates, fencing, trees, overhead wires, landscaping and how crews and material will safely reach the build location. All ASI structures are built on site.

PART 02

The practical preparation sequence

1. Confirm permissions and placement

Review applicable zoning, setbacks, HOA restrictions and permit requirements with the authority responsible for the property. Rules vary among Ohio, Kentucky and Indiana jurisdictions.

2. Plan utility locating

The person responsible for ground disturbance should coordinate the applicable 811 notification and identify private lines that a utility locating service may not mark. Ohio homeowners can consult OHIO811; use the correct one-call system for the actual state.

3. Confirm water management

Establish a site-specific route for surface water away from the building and adjoining property. Downspouts, drainage swales and neighboring grades may affect the solution.

4. Prepare the supporting surface

Where appropriate, remove unsuitable organic material, address weak soil, shape and compact the subgrade, and install the specified base and foundation system. Stone type, thickness, fabric and moisture-control details are project-specific.

5. Review elevations and access

Agree on final support elevations, steps or ramps, and maintenance clearances before proceeding with construction.

PART 03

What is included in a standard ASI base?

Standard support arrangement

The approved traditional assembly uses 4×6 pressure-treated foundation beams and 16×16×4-inch solid concrete support pads, with layout and anchorage determined for building size and loading.

Leveling allowance

Up to 6 inches of out-of-level condition is included under the current ASI policy; additional leveling and site preparation require a separate written quotation. This is not a guarantee that every sloping site can be built without further work.

Know the boundary of the proposal

Clearing, grading, extensive excavation, drainage remediation and other property work should be identified explicitly in the written scope rather than assumed.

PART 04

Questions to settle before a proposal

Where does stormwater go?

Ask what happens to the chosen location during heavy rain and where runoff will leave the site.

Who prepares the pad?

Confirm the party responsible for access, clearing, soil work, base materials and any separate foundation engineering.

Will use change the support design?

Heavy equipment, concentrated loads, finished interiors and specialty uses may require different design and review.

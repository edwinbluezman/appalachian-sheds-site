THE ESSENTIAL ANSWER

What should you know before choosing?

Factory shed ramps can be much less expensive because they are short, standardized accessories. Appalachian Sheds planning ranges assume a site-built access system coordinated with actual finished floor height, door width, foundation elevation, equipment use, drainage and safe bearing at the ramp base.

Pressure-treated wood remains the practical standard for mower, garden-cart and general equipment access. The ramp should be framed as a structural component, positively attached at the building and supported on a stable approach surface.

PART 01

Standard Pressure-Treated Equipment Ramps

Pressure-treated wood remains the practical standard for mower, garden-cart and general equipment access. The ramp should be framed as a structural component, positively attached at the building and supported on a stable approach surface.

Ramp width should follow the actual clear door opening and the widest item that must cross it—not simply the nominal door size.

Wide / Reinforced Equipment Ramps

Zero-turn mowers, compact tractors, ATVs, loaded hand trucks and heavier shop equipment require more than a wider deck board surface. Ramp framing, joist spacing, attachment, lower bearing and wheel-path loading should all be selected around the actual equipment.

A reinforced ramp should be specified by intended load and geometry rather than simply labeled “heavy-duty.”

Residential-Style Entry Steps & Landings

A fiberglass entry door, office door or studio entrance often needs steps rather than an equipment ramp. Steps should be laid out around the actual threshold elevation, door swing and approach direction.

Where a landing is required or simply improves usability, it should be large enough to provide a stable pause area instead of forcing the user to operate the door while standing on a stair tread.

Premium Composite / Architectural Access

Composite decking can reduce routine surface maintenance and provide a more finished appearance at high-visibility office, studio, pool-house or custom-building entries. The structural frame still needs to be designed for the ramp geometry and load.

Composite surface selection should include a traction review because not all boards behave the same way on a slope when wet, frosted or shaded.

PART 02

Accessibility-Oriented Ramp Planning

Convenience access and code-reviewed accessibility are not the same thing. A ramp intended for mobility use can require substantially more length, landings, handrails, edge protection, clear width and site area than a mower ramp.

Concrete & Permanent Approach Systems

Concrete can provide a durable, permanent approach where equipment traffic is frequent or where the entry is integrated with a sidewalk, patio or larger hardscape plan. It is also less forgiving than wood if the finished floor elevation or drainage plan is wrong.

Concrete access should be coordinated after the final building threshold height and drainage elevation are known.

Code & Safety Reality: Ramp Geometry Depends on the Type of Access

For ordinary shed equipment ramps, the practical slope is selected around the equipment, traction and available space. For residential-code ramps and mobility-oriented access, slope, landing and handrail rules can apply. Local requirements should be confirmed before the final scope is approved.

Choose the Access System by Use — Not by Appearance Alone

The entry should be comfortable for what moves through it most often.

PART 03

Plan the Door, Floor Height and Access Path as One System

Appalachian Sheds can coordinate door width, finished floor height, ramp or step geometry, landing space, site grade, drainage and the equipment or people who will use the entry before the final written scope is approved.

IMPORTANT PROJECT DISTINCTION

How this relates to an Appalachian Sheds project

Built on your property

All Appalachian Sheds buildings are constructed on site. Property access, drainage, topography, location restrictions and the selected model can affect planning.

Exterior shell versus finished interior

Our standard building proposal describes a weather-tight exterior shell. Insulation, electrical, plumbing, heating, cooling, and interior wall or ceiling finishes are separate work unless expressly listed in the written scope.

Only the written scope is authoritative

Model-specific specifications, pricing, selected options, finishing requirements and any engineering or permit obligations must be confirmed for the actual building and site. Avoid treating this general guide as an engineering design or a permit determination.

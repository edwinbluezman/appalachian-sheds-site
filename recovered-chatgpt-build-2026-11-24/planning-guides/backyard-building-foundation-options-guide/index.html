Backyard Building Foundation Options Guide

A foundation should be selected for the site and the building loads—not just the appearance or price of the materials.

THE ESSENTIAL ANSWER

What should you know?

The best foundation is the one that safely supports the planned loads, maintains alignment, manages water and fits the soil and slope. An ordinary storage shed, a heavily equipped workshop and a finished studio may need different structural and site planning.

PART 01

What a foundation must accomplish

Support the full load path

Roof, wall, floor and stored loads must transfer into foundations and ultimately suitable supporting ground. Point loads and concentrated equipment loads deserve special attention.

Manage moisture

Avoid trapping runoff beneath the floor and maintain appropriate clearance for siding, framing and ventilation. The right approach depends on the foundation type and site drainage.

Stay stable over time

A building can appear level at installation yet still suffer from poor support, unsuitable fill or water-driven settlement.

PART 02

The approved ASI traditional floor and support system

Foundation beams and pads

The current standard specifies 4×6 pressure-treated beams on 16×16×4-inch solid concrete pads. Support locations and spacing are based on building size and expected loads; no universal pad grid is specified.

Floor framing and decking

Traditional model floor joists are 2×6 pressure-treated lumber at 16 inches on center, with ¾-inch pressure-treated exterior-rated plywood decking.

Anchorage and connections

Anchoring and connection details are engineered or selected according to building size and applicable loads. The proposal and local approvals govern the final arrangement.

Leveling versus site preparation

An out-of-level condition of up to 6 inches is included. More extensive leveling, drainage corrections, additional supports or special engineering must be addressed in writing.

PART 03

Foundation alternatives worth discussing

Prepared base with supported beams

Suitable where soil, drainage, bearing and building design permit. The base and the actual point-support arrangement need to be evaluated together.

Pier or engineered footing system

May be considered for grades, frost or load conditions that require deeper or differently distributed support. Design, spacing and depth depend on site and jurisdiction.

Concrete slab

May suit certain higher-load or specialized uses, subject to site conditions, drainage, anchorage and applicable design requirements. A slab is not automatically included in ASI model prices.

PART 04

Warning signs that call for a property review

Persistent standing water or nearby swales

Investigate water movement and whether placement should change.

Soft fill, recently disturbed soil or noticeable settlement

Assess the supporting soil and site preparation rather than relying on surface appearance.

Large slope or heavy concentrated equipment

Discuss engineered support design and whether the standard foundation concept remains suitable.

Office, sauna or other specialty use

Consider intended occupancy, loads, utilities, weather protection and the different code implications of the actual use.

Continue planning

Use the relevant guide or option page to narrow your choices, then request a written proposal or property walk-through when your property, intended use or building design requires a site-specific decision.

What should you know before choosing?

LP SmartSide Trim

Best for many painted Appalachian Sheds buildings when the trim is coordinated with compatible siding, fascia, rake boards, corners and opening casing.

Best fit: classic storage buildings, workshops, garden buildings and painted custom structures.

Cellular PVC Trim

Best where moisture resistance, refined appearance and lower maintenance are priorities—especially on offices, studios, pool houses, porch details and fascia-heavy elevations.

Important: expansion, joints, fastening and adhesive details must be handled correctly.

Western Red Cedar Trim

Best for Appalachian-inspired character, stained accents, cottage details, porch elements and highly visible natural trim areas.

Maintenance reality: cedar requires a realistic stain, sealer, paint or intentional weathering plan.

PART 02

Compare Trim Materials Before Building the Exterior Package

Trim cost changes with material, board width, roofline complexity, number of openings, finish system, caulking requirements and whether the work is part of the original build or added later.

Trim Is Priced by Linear Foot or Defined Package Scope.

Exact pricing should be confirmed after the building size, roofline, openings, material, finish and written scope are known.

These figures are planning ranges—not quotes. Large buildings, taller walls, steep roof pitches, complex gables, premium materials, multi-color finish packages and post-build upgrades require scope review.

Every Trim Position Has a Different Job

Fascia, rake, frieze, corner boards, casing and band boards are not interchangeable. Each position affects appearance, weather detailing and finish maintenance differently.

Proportion Matters as Much as Material.

Trim that is too narrow can look unfinished. Trim that is too wide can overpower a smaller building.

PART 03

The Trim Is Installed Late — But the Trim Plan Starts Early

Exterior trim should be defined before siding cuts, finish colors, opening casing, gable accents and roofline details are finalized.

Trim Decisions Affect Other Exterior Work.

Changing trim late can affect siding layout, paint lines, caulking, window and door balance, gable appearance and roof-edge depth.

The Best Trim Package Depends on the Building

A practical storage building, garden cottage, office, pool house and custom specialty structure should not automatically receive the same trim treatment.

Match the Trim to the Architecture.

The goal is not “more trim.” The goal is correct proportion, material and finish for the structure.

PART 04

Trim Should Not Be Chosen Separately From the Exterior Package

Trim is where siding, paint, stain, doors, windows, rooflines, accents, porches and caulking systems meet.

Exterior Systems Must Work Together.

A trim choice can be attractive by itself and still be wrong for the selected siding profile, roofline or maintenance expectation.

Weather, Caulking & Maintenance Reality

Trim creates many of the seams, corners, transitions and exposed edges that must be detailed correctly and inspected over time. The goal is to seal the joints that should be sealed without blocking intentional water-shedding or drainage paths.

PART 05

Higher Cost Does Not Automatically Mean Better

The correct trim material is the one that matches the siding system, exposure, finish, intended use, architecture and maintenance expectation.

Specify the Material for the Job.

Standard storage, premium offices, poolside buildings and natural-character structures can legitimately call for different trim systems.

LP SmartSide Trim

Often makes sense when paired with compatible siding and a painted finish system.

May make sense for fascia, poolside buildings, finished offices, porch details and lower-maintenance expectations.

Western Red Cedar

Provides authentic warmth and character when the owner accepts the staining, sealing or weathering path.

PART 07

Why Fascia & Trim Pricing Is Shown as Planning Ranges

Trim pricing changes with material, board width, building size, wall height, roof pitch, gable complexity, fascia depth, number of windows and doors, finish scope, caulking scope, material availability and installation access. Appalachian Sheds uses planning ranges to establish realistic expectations, then confirms exact pricing in the written project scope.

IMPORTANT PROJECT DISTINCTION

How this relates to an Appalachian Sheds project

Built on your property

All Appalachian Sheds buildings are constructed on site. Property access, drainage, topography, location restrictions and the selected model can affect planning.

Exterior shell versus finished interior

Our standard building proposal describes a weather-tight exterior shell. Insulation, electrical, plumbing, heating, cooling, and interior wall or ceiling finishes are separate work unless expressly listed in the written scope.

Only the written scope is authoritative

Model-specific specifications, pricing, selected options, finishing requirements and any engineering or permit obligations must be confirmed for the actual building and site. Avoid treating this general guide as an engineering design or a permit determination.

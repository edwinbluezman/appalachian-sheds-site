THE ESSENTIAL ANSWER

What should you know before choosing?

The best skylight installation is designed into the building from the start. That allows the opening, headers, rafter layout, flashing kit, roofing, insulation and light shaft to be coordinated before the roof is closed.

ASI position: use skylights selectively. One well-placed unit with the correct glass and shade can improve a finished office or studio more than several poorly placed roof openings.

PART 01

Plan the Skylight Before the Roof Is Framed.

Manual Venting Skylight

Opens to release warm air and can support natural airflow when a deliberate lower-air inlet is available.

Solar / Powered Venting Skylight

Useful for vaulted ceilings or finished spaces where manual operation is inconvenient. Controls and rain-sensing features vary by product.

Tubular Daylight System

Delivers daylight through a reflective tube with much less roof opening than a full skylight. Excellent for workshops, storage and small interior zones.

PART 02

Curb-Mounted Skylight

Raised-curb installation useful when roof slope, roofing material or manufacturer requirements make a curb-mounted system more appropriate.

Glass, Shades & Controls

Low-E performance, safety glazing, room-darkening or light-filtering shades, manual rods and powered controls are selected by use case.

Finished Light Shaft

For finished ceilings, the shaft must be framed, insulated, air-sealed and finished as part of the building envelope.

Custom / Multiple Skylights

Multiple openings, large units, metal roofs, unusual spacing and specialty roof geometry require coordinated framing and flashing review.

PART 03

The Skylight Must Be Approved for the Actual Roof Pitch

Roof pitch is not a cosmetic detail. Manufacturers engineer skylight bodies and flashing systems for specific slope ranges and roofing materials. The exact product instructions—not a generic shed rule—control the installation.

Deck-Mount and Curb-Mount Are Not Interchangeable Labels.

Current VELUX manual deck-mounted skylights list a 14°–85° roof-pitch range. Current pitched Sun Tunnel products commonly list 14°–60°. Curb-mounted systems can address lower-slope conditions, subject to the exact product and flashing system.

Choose the Glass by U-Factor, Solar Heat Gain and Safety — Not by Marketing Name Alone

ENERGY STAR skylight qualification is based on NFRC-certified performance and varies by climate zone. U-Factor measures heat transfer; SHGC measures solar heat admitted through the glazing. Lower numbers generally mean less heat transfer or solar gain.

Skylight Glazing Has Special Safety Requirements.

IRC skylight / sloped-glazing provisions identify laminated, tempered and other approved safety-glazing materials. ASI should specify a listed skylight product rather than ordinary window glass placed overhead.

Measures thermal transfer through the complete skylight. Lower U-Factor generally improves resistance to heat loss / gain through the unit.

PART 04

Design Where the Light Lands — Not Just Where the Skylight Fits Between Rafters

Toplighting can deliver significant daylight, but direct sun can also create glare and cooling load. Low-E glazing and shades help control solar gain, and the interior light shaft influences how broadly light spreads through the room.

Check the Screen, Desk and Workbench Layout First.

A skylight directly over a computer monitor, detailed workbench or seating area can be less comfortable than one positioned to wash light across the room.

The Flashing System Is Part of the Skylight — Not an Optional Accessory

Manufacturer-compatible flashing must match both the skylight and roof covering. Current VELUX deck-mounted systems, for example, use different flashing packages for asphalt shingles / shakes / slate, tile and metal roofing, with adhesive underlayment incorporated into specified systems.

Do Not Improvise the Roof Opening.

Use the listed flashing system, required underlayment, specified fasteners and manufacturer clearances for the selected unit.

Use a compatible step-flashing system and manufacturer-required underlayment around the opening.

PART 05

New-Build ASI Skylights Should Usually Cost Less Than Cutting Into an Existing Finished Roof

Separate Unit / Roof Scope From Interior Finish Scope.

The skylight opening and weatherproofing belong here. Premium T&G, drywall or other finished-ceiling work should coordinate with the Interior Finish category when the scope extends beyond a basic shaft.

Planning ranges are not fixed quotes. Exact price follows the selected skylight, roof material, pitch, framing, interior finish and project jurisdiction.

An Operable Skylight Is One Air Opening — Not a Complete Moisture-Control System

A venting skylight can exhaust warm air when replacement air enters lower in the building, but it does not replace dehumidification, an ERV, bath / process exhaust or the roof-assembly ventilation strategy where those systems are required.

Ventilation & Moisture Control

Use that category for whole-building humidity, fresh air, roof ventilation and vapor-control strategy.

PART 06

Adding a Roof Opening Can Be a Structural Alteration

The City of Cincinnati requires permits for new construction, major repairs, alterations and additions that include structural changes. Cincinnati also has a narrow permit exemption for qualifying storage sheds not greater than 100 square feet and no higher than eight feet average height, but that exemption should not be assumed to cover every skylight or roof-framing alteration.

Confirm the Project Address.

Ohio, Northern Kentucky and Southeast Indiana jurisdictions can use different permit, energy and structural requirements. Final requirements follow the property location and adopted code.

IMPORTANT PROJECT DISTINCTION

How this relates to an Appalachian Sheds project

Built on your property

All Appalachian Sheds buildings are constructed on site. Property access, drainage, topography, location restrictions and the selected model can affect planning.

Exterior shell versus finished interior

Our standard building proposal describes a weather-tight exterior shell. Insulation, electrical, plumbing, heating, cooling, and interior wall or ceiling finishes are separate work unless expressly listed in the written scope.

Only the written scope is authoritative

Model-specific specifications, pricing, selected options, finishing requirements and any engineering or permit obligations must be confirmed for the actual building and site. Avoid treating this general guide as an engineering design or a permit determination.

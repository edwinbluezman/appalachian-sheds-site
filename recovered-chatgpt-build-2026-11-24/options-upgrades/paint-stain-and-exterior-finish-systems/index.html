THE ESSENTIAL ANSWER

What should you know before choosing?

LP SmartSide and DuraTemp belong in a premium exterior paint conversation. Natural cedar and pine can support stain systems when the wood, exposure and maintenance expectations are appropriate. Factory-colored vinyl should normally remain factory-colored unless the exact product and proposed coating are specifically reviewed.

For Appalachian Sheds, the correct sequence is simple: select the siding → confirm the compatible coating system → prepare and protect the surface → then choose the color.

One-Color Premium Paint

Best for: LP SmartSide, DuraTemp and paint-ready trim where a clean, unified exterior is desired.

Two-Color Paint Package

Best for: body color plus trim, door or architectural accent.

Solid-Color Wood Stain

Best for: natural wood where higher pigment and stronger visual coverage are desired while retaining wood texture.

PART 02

Semi-Transparent Wood Stain

Best for: cedar and pine where visible grain and warmer natural character are important.

Transparent / Clear Wood Finish

Best for: carefully selected natural-wood details where the homeowner accepts a closer maintenance cycle.

Cedar / Natural-Wood Accent Finish

Best for: brackets, shutters, flower boxes, porch details, gable accents and selected trim.

Trim / Door Accent Finish

Best for: one restrained accent color on doors, casing, fascia or architectural details.

PART 03

Recoat / Refresh

Best for: future cleaning, prep, touch-up, caulk review, repainting or restaining.

Planning: site-specific; existing finish condition usually matters more than building age alone.

Not Every Finish Belongs on Every Siding

The coating must match the product underneath it. This is especially important with engineered wood and factory-colored vinyl, where manufacturer instructions should control the final finish decision.

LP SmartSide Is a Paint Path — Not a Stain Path.

Current LP application instructions call for a high-quality exterior paint and specifically say not to use stain on LP SmartSide products.

A Premium Finish Starts Before the First Coat

Paint and stain cannot compensate for wet material, exposed fibers, poorly prepared cuts, dirt, incompatible sealant or incorrect joint detailing.

PART 04

Clean, Dry, Compatible and Properly Detailed.

Those four conditions matter more than choosing the most expensive coating.

Surface Area + Trim Detail + Prep + Color Count

Those four variables produce a more credible finish price than shed floor area alone.

These ranges are for planning. Final written pricing depends on actual finish surface, material compatibility, preparation, coating system and weather window.

More Pigment Generally Means More Visual Coverage and Protection

Exterior stain is not one product category. Transparent, semi-transparent, semi-solid and solid stains produce different appearances and different maintenance expectations.

PART 05

Visible Grain Comes With More Maintenance Responsibility.

The more natural wood the customer wants to see, the more closely the finish condition should be monitored.

Premium Color Design Is Usually Restrained

For most Appalachian Sheds buildings, one body color plus one controlled trim or door accent will look more intentional than several competing colors.

Siding → Roof → Trim → Doors / Windows → Accent Color

Color should be the final coordination step, not the first design decision.

Use related body and trim colors when the shed should feel like a natural extension of the primary residence.

PART 06

Choose the Correct Finish System Before Choosing the Color

Appalachian Sheds can coordinate siding, trim, doors, roof color, natural-wood accents, preparation, caulking and coating type so the final exterior looks intentional and has a maintenance path the homeowner understands.

IMPORTANT PROJECT DISTINCTION

How this relates to an Appalachian Sheds project

Built on your property

All Appalachian Sheds buildings are constructed on site. Property access, drainage, topography, location restrictions and the selected model can affect planning.

Exterior shell versus finished interior

Our standard building proposal describes a weather-tight exterior shell. Insulation, electrical, plumbing, heating, cooling, and interior wall or ceiling finishes are separate work unless expressly listed in the written scope.

Only the written scope is authoritative

Model-specific specifications, pricing, selected options, finishing requirements and any engineering or permit obligations must be confirmed for the actual building and site. Avoid treating this general guide as an engineering design or a permit determination.

CURRENT PLANNING GUIDANCE

Exterior finishing is a time-sensitive responsibility

ASI exterior shells may include unfinished or factory-primed siding and natural unfinished cedar trim, depending on model and selected materials. Final painting or staining is optional and separately priced unless explicitly included.

ASI’s current policy requires appropriate exterior painting or staining within 30 days. Roseburg also recommends an appropriate solid-color acrylic-latex finish on DuraTemp within 30 days of installation; follow the selected product and coating manufacturers’ instructions.

Clarify who will finish the building, what surface preparation is included, and whether weather delays or site access affect the schedule.

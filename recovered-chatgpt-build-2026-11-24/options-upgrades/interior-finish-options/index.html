THE ESSENTIAL ANSWER

What should you know before choosing?

A 10×12 shed does not contain only 120 square feet of finish work. Four walls, gable areas and the ceiling can easily create several times that much finish surface. Appalachian Sheds therefore prices wall and ceiling materials by actual installed surface area, then adds trim, coatings, flooring-readiness and detail work according to the selected package.

PART 01

Interior Finish Pricing Should Be Based on the Actual Wall and Ceiling Surfaces — Not the Shed Floor Area Alone.

Durable Utility Interior

Best for workshops, tool rooms, garden buildings and active storage where wall strength, cleanability and durability matter more than residential appearance.

Typical scope: plywood or durable utility panels, sealed / painted surfaces, simple trim, electrical coordination and floor-finish readiness.

Office & Studio Interior

Best for backyard offices, studios, hobby rooms and clean flex spaces where a smoother, room-like interior is desired.

Typical scope: drywall or smooth panel walls, painted ceiling, trim, paint, lighting coordination, flooring readiness and comfort-system coordination.

Appalachian Wood Interior

Best for premium retreats, studios, pool houses and custom buildings where warm wood character and detailed finish work are central to the design.

Typical scope: pine T&G, beadboard or mixed-material surfaces, upgraded trim, clear / painted finishes, accent details and coordinated lighting.

PART 02

Know What Each Finish Material Costs Before Building the Package

The most accurate way to price a finished interior is to price each wall and ceiling system by actual surface area, then add trim and finish details separately.

Small Buildings Can Cost More Per Square Foot.

Mobilization, corners, window and door casing, gable cuts, ceiling work and trim labor do not shrink in direct proportion to the shed footprint.

Finish-square-foot pricing refers to actual wall or ceiling area being covered, not the building footprint.

The Finish Goes On Late — But the Finish Plan Starts Early

Interior finish work should not begin until the shell, rough-ins and insulation strategy are ready. This prevents finished surfaces from being opened later to move wiring, add blocking or correct comfort problems.

Rough-In First. Finish Last.

The most expensive interior mistakes are usually sequencing mistakes rather than material mistakes.

PART 03

The Best Interior Is the One That Matches the Work Happening Inside

A workshop and a backyard office should not receive the same wall system. Durability, sound, lighting, humidity, storage and appearance priorities are different.

Use Comes Before Appearance.

Select the performance needs first, then choose the finish character.

Design the Interior Before We Cover the Framing

Appalachian Sheds can coordinate the wall finish, ceiling finish, trim, lighting, storage, insulation, HVAC readiness and flooring plan so the finished space performs as well as it looks.

IMPORTANT PROJECT DISTINCTION

How this relates to an Appalachian Sheds project

Built on your property

All Appalachian Sheds buildings are constructed on site. Property access, drainage, topography, location restrictions and the selected model can affect planning.

Exterior shell versus finished interior

Our standard building proposal describes a weather-tight exterior shell. Insulation, electrical, plumbing, heating, cooling, and interior wall or ceiling finishes are separate work unless expressly listed in the written scope.

Only the written scope is authoritative

Model-specific specifications, pricing, selected options, finishing requirements and any engineering or permit obligations must be confirmed for the actual building and site. Avoid treating this general guide as an engineering design or a permit determination.

MAKE THE NEXT DECISION

Your next planning step

Decide whether the intended use requires a finished, conditioned or utility-served space. Record that use before pricing the shell and any separately scoped interior work.

Roof Pitch Selection Guide

Compare roof slopes and understand how roof pitch affects appearance, interior volume and the character of the building.

THE ESSENTIAL ANSWER

What should you know before choosing?

How roof angle changes appearance, height, loft space, weather shedding, material use, and the overall character of your backyard building

Roof pitch is one of the most influential design decisions on a backyard building. It changes far more than the shape of the roof. Pitch affects overall height, interior volume, potential loft space, roof surface area, weather movement, and how naturally the finished structure relates to your home.

Select roof pitch as part of the complete building

How roof pitch changes the building

Roof pitch measures inches of vertical rise per 12 inches of horizontal run. A 9/12 gable has different proportions and wall-to-ridge rise than a 5/12 gable of the same span. Model architecture, building width, total height and allowable site height all matter.

ASI approved roof profiles

Carriage Lane: 5/12 gable. Country Cottage with Shed Dormer: 9/12 main roof. Urban Studio: 2/12 single slope. Appalachian Farmhouse: 8/12. Pine Ridge Shed: 8/12. Laurel Ridge: 2/12 mono-slope attached and stand-alone. Nordic Retreat: 10/12. Foxfire Cottage: 9/12. Grand Depot: 9/12. Appalachian Barn: gambrel upper 7/12 and lower 20/12. Appalachian Firewood Shed: 5/12. The Antique Saltbox geometry is confirmed by the final model design rather than assigned a new pitch here.

A low-slope roof is a technical detail, not just a style

For slopes between 2/12 and less than 4/12, ARMA identifies special underlayment approaches for asphalt shingles. Product-specific requirements and the applicable building code control the final system. A 2/12 roof cannot automatically receive ordinary steep-slope asphalt shingle detailing.

What a steeper roof can change

Steeper roofs can increase the building’s visible height and available roof volume. However, usable loft space depends on framing, floor design, access, headroom and loads—not pitch alone. More roof material and labor may affect the written proposal.

Questions to resolve before finalizing

Confirm intended use, local height and setback limitations, roof-water management, venting, selected covering and manufacturer application limits. Ask for the model-specific design and proposed roof assembly to be confirmed in writing.

IMPORTANT PROJECT DISTINCTION

How this relates to an Appalachian Sheds project

Built on your property

All Appalachian Sheds buildings are constructed on site. Property access, drainage, topography, location restrictions and the selected model can affect planning.

Exterior shell versus finished interior

Our standard building proposal describes a weather-tight exterior shell. Insulation, electrical, plumbing, heating, cooling, and interior wall or ceiling finishes are separate work unless expressly listed in the written scope.

Only the written scope is authoritative

Model-specific specifications, pricing, selected options, finishing requirements and any engineering or permit obligations must be confirmed for the actual building and site. Avoid treating this general guide as an engineering design or a permit determination.

CURRENT PLANNING GUIDANCE

Low-slope roofing requires a separate installation decision

Roof pitch is part of a particular model’s architectural identity, not a universal upgrade that can be swapped without review. The Urban Studio and both Laurel Ridge configurations have an approved 2/12 roof form; other models have different approved slopes.

ARMA describes special underlayment procedures for asphalt shingles on slopes from 2/12 to less than 4/12. The final assembly must also be permitted by the chosen shingle manufacturer and applicable code. Do not assume ordinary steep-slope shingle installation is suitable.

When comparing roof shapes, confirm the complete assembly, drainage, overall height, ventilation and engineered framing—not just the appearance of the roofline.
